Buckman Brewhouse: A Brewery in the Old Fire Station

The story

Buckman Brewhouse is a family-owned brewery, bakery, and restaurant operating in Cabot's old Central Fire Station at 114 W. Pine Street — a building the city owns and leases to the business. Getting there took a tie-breaking mayoral vote on the lease, a planning-commission denial, a three-hour appeal hearing, a second tie-break, a year of construction, an uncomfortable visit from a council member, and, after opening, a fight with state alcohol law over growlers. The Robbins family had been selling bread at the Cabot Farmers Market in 2024 before any of it began (The Leader, Feb. 25, 2026).

  • - September 24, 2024 — At a special council meeting, the council takes up Resolution 10-A of 2024, authorizing the mayor and clerk-treasurer to lease 114 W. Pine Street to "Buckman Brewery." The vote ties 4-4, with Council Members Maggie Cope, Brandon Hillenburg, Stephen Redd, and Ron Waymack voting no; Mayor Ken Kincade casts the deciding vote in favor (The Leader, Dec. 4, 2024, and Jan. 8, 2025). The lease attached to the resolution (Exhibit A) sets a 20-year term running to 2044, extendable by joint agreement for another 20 years. Its rent structure is unusual: during the first ten years, instead of cash rent, the tenant must complete at least $600,000 in repairs and improvements — electrical, plumbing, HVAC, structural work, parking-lot repairs, and whatever else brings the building up to code — entirely at the tenant's expense, with all improvements becoming city property and quarterly reports owed to the city. After the first ten years, rent becomes $8,200 per month in cash. The building is leased as-is, for use only as a brewery and restaurant.
  • - November 2024 — The Planning Commission votes 5-0 to deny the special-use permit the brewhouse needs, over parking. The building has no parking of its own; the city code requires 34 spaces, and the mayor's offered solution is an off-site parking agreement for the City Annex lot at 208 N. First St. Chairman Chad Young recuses himself. Owner Christopher Robbins describes a 102-seat restaurant, about ten employees, lunch and dinner service Tuesday through Saturday, with the entrance and deliveries through the alley (The Leader, Dec. 4, 2024).
  • - January 6, 2025 — The council holds a three-hour appeal hearing at the brewhouse's request. A motion to uphold the Planning Commission's denial ties 4-4 — Cope, Hillenburg, Pickard, and Redd to uphold; Ed Long, James Reid, Corey Spangler, and Matt Webber against — and fails when the mayor, who can break a tie only with a yes vote, does not rescue it. A second motion, to send the permit back to the Planning Commission for further review, also ties 4-4 the other way, and this time Mayor Kincade votes yes to break the tie (The Leader, Jan. 8, 2025).
  • - January 28, 2025 — On remand, the Planning Commission grants the special-use permit, 4-2-1, on a motion by Commissioner Chuck Prater — who had spoken against the parking arrangement in November — seconded by Commissioner Shelia DeArmond, with Chairman Young again recused (Planning Commission minutes in the Feb. 3, 2025 council packet).
  • - 2025: the build-out year. By July the brewing equipment is installed, the business is hiring, and its application to sell alcohol on the premises is working through the process (The Leader, July 2, 2025); legal notices for the private-club permit application run in the paper that month. The May 5, 2025 council packet includes the business's menu. In his March 2025 State of the City address, Mayor Kincade lists Buckman Brewhouse among the restaurants coming to Cabot (The Leader, March 26, 2025).
  • - December 1, 2025 — Council Member Stephen Redd asks for a report on the progress of Buckman Brewery (council minutes).
  • - December 2, 2025 — Council Member Kyle Pickard enters the under-construction brewhouse through a side door, unannounced, saying he is checking on progress. According to the incident report business manager Joshua Smith filed with Cabot Police on Dec. 4, the visit turned hostile over a group photograph hung on the wall that included Pickard's father — the late Carl Pickard, a Cabot firefighter and later fire chief (1962–1995) who, his son said, did not believe in drinking. Smith told police Pickard entered an active construction site without authorization, made him feel unsafe, and acted inappropriately and unprofessionally; Smith said he did not want to press charges but filed for documentation. The Leader reached out to Pickard for comment; he did not return the call before publication (The Leader, Dec. 10, 2025).
  • - February 2026 — Buckman Brewhouse officially opens. The Cabot Chamber of Commerce holds a ribbon cutting on Friday, Feb. 20, 2026; the Leader calls it "the long-awaited restaurant and brewery," open "after an extended period of jumping through legal hoops, council approvals, delays and controversies," with its dining room in the old fire-truck bay (The Leader, Feb. 25, 2026).
  • - April 2026: the growler fight. The brewhouse announces it cannot sell growlers — refillable bottles of its beer — for customers to take home (arkansasliquor.com, April 15, 2026; THV11 also covers the Cabot alcohol debate). The state ABC private-club license that lets it serve alcohol in dry Lonoke County covers on-premises consumption only; under state law, microbrewery-restaurant private clubs in dry counties may not make off-premises sales, while breweries in wet counties can sell growlers through a retail beer permit with a growler endorsement. Owner Kim Robbins tells The Leader the business was surprised to learn carryout sales were not allowed, and asks state legislators to amend the law so dry-county breweries can sell their own on-site-made beer in growlers — emphasizing the ask is narrow: "We're not out to change the law to turn Lonoke County wet." Under their private-club model, growlers would be filled by the ounce for members only (The Leader, April 29, 2026).
  • - October 28, 2026 — The Leader's "City Properties" survey of city-owned buildings summarizes the lease as it now stands: a 20-year lease that may be extended; at least $600,000 in repairs and improvements required in the first ten years; $5,000 per month in rent paid not to the city but to Southern Bank, holder of the brewhouse's Small Business Administration loan, until that loan is paid in full — the SBA funds being used for renovations in lieu of rent to the city; after the loan is repaid, rent rises to $8,200 per month, paid to the city and into its general fund; quarterly expense reports required; loan terms reassessed against the value added to the property each year; and if the brewhouse were to fold in three years, the city would keep the building improvements for the next renter, with fixtures and additions as city property and no city obligation on the brewery's leased equipment.

Where it stands now. The brewhouse is open and active — menus, weekend specials, events such as a four-course Oktoberfest dinner (Oct. 26, 2026), a "Brew Society" mug club, and hiring posts appear in local groups through October 2026. The growler question awaits the state legislature; no change in the law is reported in this corpus.

What critics said

  • - The lease itself. Half the council opposed it in September 2024 — Cope, Hillenburg, Redd, and Waymack voted no, and it passed only on the mayor's tie-break (The Leader, Dec. 4, 2024). The coverage in this corpus does not record each member's stated reasons at that meeting; the arguments that followed concentrated on parking, location, and alcohol rather than on the lease's financial terms.
  • - Parking and pedestrian safety (Planning Commission, November 2024). Commissioner Chuck Prater argued that places like this never have enough parking and objected to drinkers crossing the street to the city lot. Commissioner Liz Bird raised traffic safety near the railroad tracks and the traffic light, where brewery traffic would mix with school dismissal and dinner hours. Commissioner Shelia DeArmond noted that if the City Annex were leased out, the shared parking could disappear (The Leader, Dec. 4, 2024).
  • - The next-door neighbor. Attorney Patrick Melikian, whose law office adjoins the old fire house, opposed the permit at both the commission and the council appeal: the off-site parking would burden existing businesses, closing the alley would cost him his handicapped parking spaces and require a new sidewalk and entrance, the alley is uneven with a concrete drainage ditch and a two-foot drop-off, and intoxicated patrons would be walking through it (The Leader, Dec. 4, 2024; Jan. 8, 2025).
  • - "A dry county and a dry city." Former Cabot Fire Chief Phil Robinson opposed the permit on principle: Lonoke County's voters chose dry status long ago and reaffirmed it roughly a decade earlier, and he argued a brewery did not belong near schools, churches, and homes (The Leader, Dec. 4, 2024).
  • - "Not the right location." Council Member Brandon Hillenburg, who voted against both the lease and (initially) the permit process, told the January 2025 appeal hearing he would support the brewery at another location in Cabot but not this one (The Leader, Jan. 8, 2025).
  • - Procedure. At the same hearing, Council Member Ed Long questioned whether the Unified Development Code allows denying a special-use permit over unspecified general concerns, arguing the commission needed to cite the code; City Attorney Ben Hooper answered that a special-use permit is discretionary with the commission and not defined by a UDC code (The Leader, Jan. 8, 2025).
  • - The December 2025 visit. The brewhouse's own manager put his criticism in a police incident report: a council member entering an active construction site uninvited and berating staff over a photograph was, in his account, inappropriate and unprofessional. Pickard's side of that encounter — beyond his stated objection to his father's photo being displayed without permission in a business his father would have opposed — was not given to The Leader (Dec. 10, 2025).
  • - Facebook group chatter. In searches of Cabot Strong, WTF Cabot Arkansas, and Cabot's Petty Citizens (BCOC) in October 2026, the brewhouse appears almost entirely as a business, not a controversy: its own steady stream of specials, brunch, and event promotions, community cross-posts (paint-and-sip nights, a school-opening brunch), and a construction-progress update back on July 13, 2025 that described inspections and electrical work on the road to opening. No group thread debating the lease terms, the parking fight, or the growler issue surfaced in these searches, and the two smaller groups (Cabot Strong For Better Change, Cabot Strong (uncensored)) returned no Buckman posts at all. The policy arguments happened in hearing rooms and the newspaper; the group conversation is about the food. One small echo of the old theme: a May 2026 post from the business noted its parking lot being restriped and briefly closed — parking, still, but as customer information.

What the record shows

  • - Verified from documents: Resolution 10-A of 2024 and the full lease text (Exhibit A) in the Sept. 24, 2024 council packet minutes supplement — property (114 W. Pine St.), 20-year term to 2044 plus a possible 20-year extension, the $600,000 first-decade improvement obligation as the rent for that period, $8,200/month thereafter, quarterly reporting, brewery/restaurant-only use, as-is condition. The Planning Commission's eventual approval (4-2-1, Jan. 28, 2025) is in the Feb. 3, 2025 council packet minutes. The two 4-4 council votes and both tie-breaks are reported consistently across two Leader issues (Dec. 4, 2024; Jan. 8, 2025).
  • - The money figures conflict across sources, and this page does not settle them:
  • - The original lease text (Sept. 24, 2024) contains no cash rent at all for the first ten years — the ≥$600,000 in improvements is the rent — and no mention of any bank, SBA loan, or $5,000 payment.
  • - The Leader's Oct. 28, 2026 summary describes $5,000/month paid to Southern Bank against an SBA loan, "in lieu of" rent to the city, until the loan is repaid, with the same $8,200/month figure after. The $600,000 and $8,200 figures agree between the two sources; the $5,000/bank arrangement appears only in the 2026 summary.
  • - No lease amendment document was found in the council packets in this corpus, so when — or by what instrument — the SBA arrangement was added cannot be verified here. (Council Member Spangler's own published write-ups on the Buckman lease amendments exist outside this corpus and were not used; anyone reconciling the figures should start from the executed lease and any amendments at City Hall.) What the record does show is that the numbers a resident finds depend entirely on which document they read — the 2024 lease or the 2026 summary — and those documents do not describe the same payment stream for the renovation years.
  • - The SBA loan's total amount does not appear in any corpus source.
  • - A naming note: the 2024 resolution and lease say "Buckman Brewery"; the business trades as "Buckman Brewhouse." Christopher Robbins appears as owner in the 2024–25 hearings; Kim Robbins is quoted as owner in April 2026. The Leader describes the business as family-owned.
  • - The parking numbers were specific, and both sides used them: the code requirement was 34 spaces (the fire station has none); a parking exhibit in the Jan. 27, 2025 council packet breaks the demand down as Buckman 34, the courts 30, and Melikian 26, against roughly 75 spaces around the City Annex and neighboring businesses. The owners countered with photographs they said showed the lots empty at their peak dining hours (The Leader, Jan. 8, 2025).
  • - The dry-county mechanics check out as described: both the brewhouse's alcohol service and Olive Garden's (see the Olive Garden page) run through private-club licensing because Lonoke County is dry; the growler limitation follows from the private-club structure, and changing it requires state legislation, not city action (The Leader, April 29, 2026).
  • - Unknown / gaps: (1) The executed lease's start date — the Exhibit A text in the packet leaves the 2024 start and end days blank, so the precise term dates are not in this corpus. (2) The lease amendment/SBA documentation (above). (3) How much Buckman has actually spent on improvements to date against the $600,000 obligation — the lease requires quarterly reports, and Council Member Redd requested a progress report in December 2025, but no figures appear in the corpus. (4) Whether the December 2025 incident produced any council or legal follow-up. (5) The outcome of the owners' request for state growler legislation.
