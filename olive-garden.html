Olive Garden, the Sewer Line, and the Park That Hasn't Happened

The story

The Olive Garden restaurant at 232 Willie Ray Drive is open and operating — but the restaurant itself turned out to be the simple part. Attached to it is a second, stranger story: a sewer line the city ran to a piece of land next door for a mountain-bike park that was never donated, a five-figure refund fight over utility ratepayer money, and a transparency dispute over an incentive check that surfaced through a FOIA request. The would-be park parcel remains undeveloped.

The restaurant arrives (2023–2025).

  • - November 16, 2023 — The Cabot Water & Wastewater Commission votes unanimously to have its general manager work with the city on a roughly 1,450-foot, 8-inch sewer extension on Willie Ray Drive, labeled in the minutes as the "Option 2–Olive Garden" extension (minutes carried in the Dec. 18, 2023 city council packet).
  • - Late 2023 — The Planning Commission approves, 6-0, both the preliminary plat for "Lot 1, Darden Addition" (named for Olive Garden's parent company, Darden Restaurants) and the Olive Garden site plan, prepared by Richardson Engineering, with contingencies including sewer details (minutes in the Dec. 18, 2023 council packet).
  • - March 2024 — The city writes a $34,900 check from its community and economic development incentive fund to EJ Holdings of Fayetteville, the developer of the Olive Garden property, to demolish an existing building on the land. This payment became public only in September 2025 (see below).
  • - By late March 2024 — The restaurant is under construction; the Planning Commission approves a Discount Tire site plan on the parcel just east of it (The Leader, April 3, 2024).
  • - April 15, 2024 — The city council passes an ordinance approving Olive Garden's application to the state Alcoholic Beverage Control board for a private-club license to serve alcohol — the standard route for restaurants in dry Lonoke County (The Leader, April 17, 2024).
  • - August 7, 2024 — The Leader photographs the restaurant under construction: 7,825 square feet on 3.28 acres, expected to open later that year.
  • - January 2025 — The Olive Garden opens (The Leader, Feb. 4, 2026, looking back; the opening also appears in the Leader's Dec. 24, 2025 year-in-review headlines). Incoming Chamber of Commerce chairwoman Allison Tucker cited it at the February 2025 Chamber banquet as one of the large restaurants residents had long wanted (The Leader, Feb. 19, 2025).

The park and the sewer line (2024–2026).

  • - February 26, 2024 — The city council adopts Resolution No. 8 of 2024, authorizing the mayor to accept a donation of real property for a public park and to enter an agreement with the Water & Wastewater Commission to provide sewer infrastructure "to the park and surrounding area." The Leader reports the vote as 5-3, with Council Members Maggie Cope, Brandon Hillenburg, and Ron Waymack voting no. The Moyer family owned about 60 acres of undeveloped land off Willie Ray Drive next to the Olive Garden property and offered to donate six to ten acres for a mountain-bike park, under a memorandum of understanding with the Moyer Family Trust: the city would install a sewer line and build a road, Parks and Recreation would run the park, it would be named "Moyers Family Memorial Park," and the family would get its own appraisal before deciding whether to donate.
  • - Spring 2024 — The funding becomes a fight. Parks and Rec did not have the sewer money in its budget. The Advertising & Promotion (A&P) Commission declined to pay, viewing the line as an economic-development project rather than a parks project. A&P chairwoman Candace Tomboli skipped two consecutive council meetings where she had been asked to update the council on the Moyer Park sewer funding, drawing public criticism from council members (The Leader, April 17, 2024).
  • - 2024 (date not pinned in the coverage) — Cabot WaterWorks installs 1,370 feet of 8-inch sewer pipe to the parcel. The Leader reports the pipe cost $100,000, split between the city's general fund and Cabot WaterWorks. The line ties into existing sewer behind David's Burgers and Cabot Emergency Hospital; any future developer hooking into it would pay a connection fee.
  • - Before the donation was completed, the property owner died. The land went into probate. By June 2025 the last communication between the city's economic development director and the owners dated to October 2024, and the land was listed for sale; Council Member Stephen Redd noted the owners were unlikely to carve out a parcel for the city if a buyer offered $4.9 million (The Leader, June 18, 2025).
  • - May 5, 2025 — With no park and no donation, Redd proposes amending Resolution 8 of 2024 to describe the project honestly as an infrastructure sewer line for Olive Garden, to be amended back if the land ever arrives. No action is taken (The Leader, May 7, 2025).
  • - June 2025 — Some council members propose returning $50,705 to the Water & Wastewater Commission. The legal problem, as WaterWorks' own attorney warned: a utility commission may not spend ratepayer dollars on infrastructure for economic development unless it serves city property — such as a public park — and there is no park. The city, meanwhile, treats the line as an economic-development project built to help land the Olive Garden (The Leader, June 18, 2025).
  • - September 2025 — The Olive Garden surfaces again in a transparency fight. Council Member Brandon Hillenburg says a FOIA request he filed in May 2025 for Olive Garden records did not produce the March 2024 $34,900 check to EJ Holdings. Mayor Ken Kincade corrects the framing — the check was to EJ Holdings, the developer, not to Olive Garden — and the payment, for demolishing an existing building on the site, did appear in the city treasurer's general ledger and in the Move Cabot Forward presentation of October 2024, but not in the list of incentive fundings the economic development director gave the council in April 2025. Council Member Cope asks that all incentive funding be included in future reports, and the council takes a first reading on amending the ordinance to require council approval of economic-incentive funding (The Leader, Sept. 17, 2025). The episode sits inside a larger, heated meeting about FOIA compliance.
  • - February 2, 2026 — The council passes Amended Resolution No. 15 of 2025 by 6-0, ordering a refund of $50,705.40 to the Water & Wastewater Commission and amending the 2025 general fund — with a condition: the refund does not take effect if the Moyer property is acquired and dedicated as a public park by April 1, 2026. Redd says the council has "kicked this can down the road for too long." The Leader reports that Mayor Kincade threatened to veto the resolution (Feb. 4, 2026); the resolution as later published in the paper's legal notices (March 18, 2026) bears his approval. City Attorney Ben Hooper told the council he had met with a representative of the estate, which would discuss the property with family members; the park site itself he described as rough land with creeks and rock, long used as a dumping ground for stumps, logs, and concrete, with a mountain-bike park still in the Parks master plan but no cost estimate or opening date (The Leader, Feb. 4, 2026).

Where it stands now (October 2026). The restaurant is open — Olive Garden's own site lists the Cabot location as active. Development interest along the corridor continues: a Buffalo Wild Wings was announced for next door (The Leader, July 2, 2025). The Moyer parcel has produced no park. Whether the April 1, 2026 deadline was met — land donated, or refund paid — is not reported in any Leader issue in this corpus through October 28, 2026.

What critics said

  • - "A barter instead of a donation." Council Member Brandon Hillenburg voted against the February 2024 deal and described the arrangement that way from the start, because the "donation" was conditioned on the city installing a sewer line (The Leader, May 7 and June 18, 2025).
  • - Process and the A&P Commission. During the April 2024 funding fight, Council Member Corey Spangler told The Leader he supported the park project but not how the ordinance was presented, calling it inappropriate for the city to strong-arm an independent commission into paying for a project — and saying he could not blame the A&P Commission for declining to spend tax revenue collected from small local restaurants on something that appeared to benefit Darden Restaurants, a corporation he cited at $1.84 billion (The Leader, April 17, 2024). A&P chairwoman Candace Tomboli, for her part, said she stayed away from two council meetings because of her treatment at a February meeting, and the commission's attorney said questions should come through him.
  • - "We did not get the park." Council Member Stephen Redd — who voted for the resolution and says he wanted the park — became its most persistent critic once the donation stalled: the city spent the money and got a sewer line that serves Olive Garden instead, and in his words the water department was put in a misunderstanding because it believed there would be land for a park (The Leader, May 7 and June 18, 2025).
  • - Ratepayer money. The legal criticism came from Cabot WaterWorks' own attorney: ratepayer dollars cannot fund economic-development infrastructure unless it is for city property, which a never-donated park is not — the basis for the refund (The Leader, June 18, 2025; Feb. 4, 2026).
  • - Transparency. The September 2025 FOIA dispute — a council member unable to obtain an incentive check through a records request, and the check missing from the development director's list to the council — drove a push to require council approval of incentive spending (The Leader, Sept. 17, 2025).
  • - Facebook group chatter. Resident discussion of the Olive Garden itself, in searches of Cabot Strong, WTF Cabot Arkansas, and Cabot's Petty Citizens (BCOC) in October 2026, is almost entirely anticipation and everyday dining talk, not the sewer or park controversy: residents asking whether the restaurant had opened yet (October 2024, and again in February 2025, after its reported January opening), a widely discussed post praising the service during a visit marred by another diner's behavior (Feb. 6, 2025), a warning about traffic on the curve by "the new Olive Garden" (May 21, 2025), and later posts using the restaurant as a landmark — asking what was being built next to it (August 2026) and what was going in at Highway 89 and Willie Ray (July 2026). No substantive group discussion of the Moyer land, the sewer line, or the refund surfaced: a search for "Moyer" in BCOC returned no posts, and none appeared in Cabot Strong results either. The fight over the Olive Garden played out in council meetings and the newspaper, not in the groups.

What the record shows

  • - Verified from documents: the sewer-extension planning vote (Water & Wastewater Commission minutes, Nov. 16, 2023); the Darden Addition plat and Olive Garden site-plan approvals (Planning Commission minutes, late 2023); the text and agenda of Resolution No. 8 of 2024 (Feb. 26, 2024 council packet) authorizing both the land-donation acceptance and the sewer agreement; and the text of Amended Resolution No. 15 of 2025 (published as a legal notice, The Leader, March 18, 2026), which recites that the property "has not been donated to the City" and "has not been dedicated as a public park," directs the $50,705.40 refund, and suspends it if the land is acquired and dedicated by April 1, 2026. The published resolution was passed 6-0 on Feb. 2, 2026, co-sponsored by Council Members Brandon Hillenburg and Stephen Redd, and approved by Mayor Ken Kincade.
  • - The numbers do not fully agree, and this page does not reconcile them:
  • - Sewer length: 1,450 feet as planned (Water & Wastewater Commission minutes, Nov. 16, 2023) vs. 1,370 feet as installed (The Leader, May 7, 2025).
  • - Cost: The Leader reported the pipe itself cost $100,000, split $50,000 city general fund / $50,000 WaterWorks (May 7 and June 18, 2025). Its Feb. 4, 2026 account put "the cost of the project" at $137,000, funded from the general fund and the WaterWorks partnership. These may measure different scopes (pipe vs. whole project), but no source in the corpus says so explicitly.
  • - The refund: $50,705 in the Leader's news stories (June 18, 2025; Feb. 4, 2026) vs. $50,705.40 in the published resolution — and the resolution's figure for the WaterWorks contribution implies the utility's share was $50,705.40 rather than the round $50,000 the news stories reported.
  • - The incentive: $34,900 (city check to EJ Holdings, March 2024, from the city treasurer's ledger as reported Sept. 17, 2025). The development director's separate "Move Cabot Forward" accounting in the mayor's 2026 campaign coverage described $179,961 in incentives to 36 small businesses; whether the EJ Holdings payment is inside that program total is not stated in the coverage.
  • - Supported by consistent reporting: the restaurant opened in January 2025 and is operating; the donation was never completed because the owner died and the estate left the land in probate and then for sale; the city attorney maintained the signed memorandum of understanding is a binding contract the estate remains bound by, and that the city could pursue the donation in court (The Leader, June 18, 2025).
  • - One framing to treat carefully: calling the sewer line "for Olive Garden" is a characterization, not a neutral description. The authorizing resolution describes sewer "to the park and surrounding area"; the line also serves a corridor where the restaurant and other development sit, and the city has defended it as growth infrastructure with future connection fees. Council Member Redd's proposed amendment would have renamed it an Olive Garden line precisely because he believed that was its effect. Both framings are in the record.
  • - Unknown / gaps: (1) Whether the refund was actually paid after April 1, 2026, or whether the land was donated by the deadline — no Leader coverage in this corpus through Oct. 28, 2026 reports either outcome. (2) The exact date the sewer line was installed. (3) Whether the council acted on the refund proposal in July 2025, as the June 2025 coverage anticipated; the next action found is the Feb. 2, 2026 passage. (4) The current status of the Moyer estate and the land sale. (5) Whether the proposed ordinance requiring council approval of incentive funding was adopted after its September 2025 first reading.
