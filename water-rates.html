Cabot's water and sewer rate increases

The story

Cabot's water and sewer service is run by the Cabot Water and Wastewater Commission, which does business as Cabot Waterworks. The commission operates the system, but state law (Ark. Code Ann. § 14-234-214) leaves the actual rates to be fixed by the city council, by ordinance. At the end of 2024 the utility served about 27,500 people through 11,127 metered connections, led by utility manager Tim Joyner, P.E. For most of two decades, its rates barely moved — which is why the increases of 2025–2026 landed the way they did.

The long freeze. Cabot's base water rates — a flat monthly charge plus a per-1,000-gallon charge — were set by Ordinance 102 of 2005 and were still in force twenty years later: $13.50 a month for an inside-city home, plus $3.20 per 1,000 gallons for the first 5,000 gallons and $4.20 per 1,000 above that. The wastewater (sewer) rate chart dated back even further, to Ordinance 52 of 2001: $5.10 covering the first 2,000 gallons, plus $1.57 per 1,000 gallons above that. A typical sewer charge for 5,000 gallons was $9.81 a month. The one significant change in between was structural, not a general increase: Ordinance 18 of 2019 (May 20, 2019) extended wastewater service and charges to properties that use sewer but not city water, and reallocated the utility's administrative costs across customer classes.

The state steps in. Arkansas law requires water providers to justify their rates with periodic engineering rate studies (Act 605 of 2021; the commission's 2025 wastewater study also cites Act 925 of 2025). In 2025 the commission had Crist Engineers, Inc. prepare both studies: a water rate study dated August 2025 and a wastewater rate study dated September 3, 2025. Both concluded that the old rates could not carry the systems much longer.

Sewer goes first (fall 2025). The council took up the wastewater ordinance through September 2025 (second reading September 22), held the required public hearing, and adopted it on October 6, 2025 as Ordinance No. 28 of 2025. The new chart took effect in two steps:

  • - Minimum charge (first 2,000 gallons): $5.10 → $5.68 on January 1, 2026 → $6.26 on January 1, 2027
  • - Usage above 2,000 gallons: $1.57 → $2.35 → $3.12 per 1,000 gallons
  • - A 5,000-gallon sewer bill: $9.81 → $12.73 → $15.62 — about 59 percent higher over two years
  • - Sewer-only customers (no city water) saw the sharpest minimum-bill jump: $5.10 → $11.26 → $11.84
  • - Two sewer-extension areas (Briarwood/Bear Creek Phase II and Mark Lynn/Dina/Bradley) kept their higher minimums ($27.05 and $26.43) until their improvement financing expires after June 2028

The timing compounded the sticker shock: the same September 2025 meetings changed the city's trash contract (the low compliant bid worked out to $24.50 a month against a $17.95 rate that had held for 13 years), so as the Leader put it on its September 24, 2025 front page, Cabot residents were looking at increases on their garbage and sewer bills at the same time.

Then the water itself (2026). Cabot historically pumped most of its own water from wells in the Alluvial aquifer and bought only about 0.3 million gallons a day — roughly 12 percent of its supply — from Central Arkansas Water (CAW). The Arkansas Natural Resources Commission has mandated that Cabot Waterworks discontinue its groundwater wells by 2026, which means buying essentially all of its water from CAW, at CAW's wholesale rates, which are themselves on a scheduled multi-year rise. On May 4, 2026, the council adopted Ordinance No. 15 of 2026 (sponsored by the Water and Wastewater Commission; passed 8–0, with no emergency clause). It left the 2005 base water rates untouched and instead added a separate purchased-water charge on every 1,000 gallons used:

  • - $2.92 from October 1, 2026
  • - $3.37 on January 1, 2027; $3.80 in 2028; $4.18 in 2029; $4.41 in 2030; $4.66 in 2031; $4.91 on January 1, 2032

The rate study projects what that does to a typical bill: an inside-city home using 5,000 gallons paid $29.50 a month for water in 2025, rising to a projected $44.10 in 2026 and $54.05 by 2032 — an 83 percent increase over the study period, driven, in the study's words, by the pass-through of CAW's rising purchased-water fees.

The echo in council politics. The increases also produced a transparency fight. On April 20, 2026, Councilman Corey Spangler's Ordinance No. 16 of 2026 — which would have required the commission to send customers a sample December bill showing the coming charges — was tabled on Spangler's own motion, and the rate question has continued to surface in council debate since.

Where it stands (October 2026). The first sewer step has been in effect since January 1, 2026 (Cabot WaterWorks' published rate page shows the $5.68/$2.35 chart), the second sewer step arrives January 1, 2027, and the water purchased-water charge began October 1, 2026, with annual steps already adopted through 2032.

What critics said

Resident reaction concentrated less on the percentages than on the product and the process:

  • - "Fix the water first." On the morning of the October 6, 2025 hearing and vote, a post in Cabot's Petty Citizens – BCOC urged residents to attend the meeting and oppose the increase, arguing the city could not justify raising rates for water residents described as damaging to glass and fixtures. The comments (23 on the post) were dominated by water-quality complaints — extreme hardness leaving deposits on appliances, toilets and water heaters, bad taste, effects on skin and hair, filters clogging quickly — rather than by the sewer chart actually being voted on. One participant shared a tap-water contaminant lookup link; another suggested a home water softener.
  • - The correction from inside the thread. A minority in the same thread pushed back on the framing: the October 2025 proposal was wastewater only, not drinking water, and the rate study behind it was required by state law (Act 925 was named). That distinction — sewer versus water, commission versus council, state mandate versus local choice — was widely blurred in the chatter.
  • - Bill shock generally. Rate votes are not the only trigger. In February 2025, a BCOC post warning neighbors not to be shocked by post-freeze water bills drew about 75 comments of bill comparisons and explanations — a standing anxiety about high bills that predates the increases and attaches to usage spikes as readily as to rates.
  • - Affordability. In December 2025, just before the sewer increase took effect, a Cabot Strong post asked where a resident could get help paying a water bill — the increases landed on households for whom the combined utility bill was already tight.
  • - Still live in 2026. After both 2026 changes took effect, "high water bills" remained a recurring theme in the groups; an October 30, 2026 BCOC post joked about an experiment for people with high bills, treating the grievance as common knowledge.

Notably, the Facebook mining found no organized campaign against the increases comparable to the 2026 Flock camera fight — no petition drive or sustained thread — and the Leader's coverage of the October 6, 2025 meeting records the ordinance passing without reporting a public-comment confrontation at the hearing.

What the record shows

  • - The "first increase in decades" claim checks out, with one caveat. The Leader reported in September–October 2025 that the last wastewater rate change was 24 years earlier, and the rate studies confirm the base charts' vintage: wastewater from Ordinance 52 of 2001, water from Ordinance 102 of 2005. The caveat is 2019's Ordinance 18, which did change who pays wastewater charges (sewer-only customers) without raising the general chart.
  • - The 59 percent sewer figure checks out — at one usage level. At 5,000 gallons, $9.81 → $15.62 is +59 percent. The burden is uneven: the per-gallon usage charge nearly doubles by 2027 ($1.57 → $3.12), the minimum charge rises about 23 percent, and sewer-only customers' minimum more than doubles by 2027.
  • - Cabot remains cheap relative to its neighbors — on sewer, as of 2025. The Leader's comparison put the same 5,000-gallon sewer service at $31.50 in Beebe, $33.61 in Jacksonville, $37.92 in Ward and $42.46 in Austin, against Cabot's $15.62 even after the full increase. That comparison is from 2025 reporting and will age as neighbors adjust their own rates.
  • - The water increase is a pass-through, not a base-rate raise. Ordinance 15 of 2026 froze the 2005 base chart and added a per-gallon purchased-water charge keyed to buying 100 percent of supply from Central Arkansas Water after the state ordered Cabot's wells shut down by 2026. The same study modeled keeping the current partial-purchase agreement and found no increase would be needed — but that scenario sits uneasily with the well-shutdown mandate, and it is not the path the council adopted.
  • - What remains unknown. Whether CAW's actual wholesale rates through 2032 will track the study's schedule (which would justify the later steps) or come in lower (which would leave Cabot customers overpaying against a chart adopted in 2026); the corpus contains no transcript of what, if anything, residents said at the October 6, 2025 public hearing; and group chatter on this topic is sampled by search, not comprehensive — absence of a thread in the mining is not proof nobody discussed it.
