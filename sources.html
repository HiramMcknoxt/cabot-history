How this site is built

Every document and pointer here comes from a named source. Government records are public records and are reproduced in full. Copyrighted news coverage is never reproduced: the site carries a title, date, short summary and a link, and nothing more. Resident discussion from Facebook groups is paraphrased into themes; no post or comment is quoted.

The layers

  • City Council records, 2015–2026 — 700 packets, agendas and minutes from the City of Cabot's AgendaCenter and Document Center, collected into public Drive folders: 2023–2026 and 2015–2022. Scanned minutes were OCR'd so their text is searchable too. Known gaps in the city's own postings (for example, the October 19, 2026 regular meeting minutes) are gaps here as well.
  • Arkansas Leader — 466 weekly issues, August 2017–October 2026, held in a public Drive archive. The Leader is subscription content, so this site indexes issue dates and links only. A separate mentions set collects the issues that name Councilman Corey Spangler (43 issues, 2018–2026).
  • Arkansas Democrat-Gazette & local TV — first-pass indexes of Cabot coverage (KATV, THV11, KARK), links and summaries only.
  • School Board minutes — 174 sets of Cabot Public Schools board minutes, 2012–2026. Most are signed scans; their text layer is still being OCR'd, so many are listed with dates and a Drive link before their text is searchable.
  • Other city bodies — Planning Commission and Fire Pension Board minutes (Drive folder).
  • Legislative Audit — Arkansas Legislative Audit reports for the City of Cabot (Drive folder); eight reports, 1999–2024, are in hand of the 26 published.
  • City code & state law — Cabot's code of ordinances is published by the city on Municode; Arkansas acts and bills are at the Arkansas Legislature.
  • Council video — the City of Cabot's official YouTube channel is @cityofcabotarkansas9930.

A worked example: the planning-district ordinance and Act 314

On December 2, 2024, State Sen. Ricky Hill appeared before the council and urged it to pass an emergency ordinance extending the Unified Development Code's commercial rules across the city's planning district, in response to a proposed crypto-mine near the city. The council did so on February 3, 2025, adopting Ordinances No. 8, 9 and 10 of 2025. Five weeks later, on March 12, 2025, the Arkansas Senate passed HB1510 — Act 314 of 2025 — repealing municipal extraterritorial-jurisdiction (planning district) authority statewide, effective August 5, 2025. The Senate vote was 24–5; Sen. Hill voted yes. Every step of that chain is in this site's records: the December 2, 2024 meeting video (Hill speaks at about 59:28), the February 3, 2025 packets in the search index, and the bill record and Senate vote at arkleg.state.ar.us.

What residents were saying (Facebook groups)

Surveyed October 26, 2026 by targeted keyword searches in public-facing Cabot groups. Themes are paraphrased; engagement counts are as displayed at the time.

Facebook group mining for the Cabot history site (Oct 26, 2026)

Method: targeted keyword searches only (no bulk scraping). 96 queries total: 20 topics

each in Cabot Strong and WTF CABOT ARKANSAS, 20 each in Cabot's Petty Citizens – BCOC

and its backup group, and 8 each in Cabot Strong For Better Change and Cabot Strong

(uncensored). First page only per query (up to 20 posts). Bounded comment reads on

6 notable threads. Dates Central. All findings paraphrased; no post or comment text

reproduced verbatim. Group search returns loosely relevance-ranked results, not strict

keyword matches — many top results were unrelated; notable posts below were hand-filtered

for genuine relevance. Empty-content posts (no readable text, typically images or memes)

are noted where they matter.

Groups covered: Cabot Strong (~24K members); WTF CABOT ARKANSAS (~15K); Cabot's Petty

Citizens – BCOC (~11K, full topic coverage); Cabot Strong For Better Change (~500);

Cabot Strong (uncensored) (~2K). The BCOC backup group returned zero posts on all 20

queries and appears inactive or not visible to the account.

Flock / ALPR cameras

Theme: the dominant group topic of August–October 2026, sharply polarized. Early

discussion centered on organizing opposition and the cameras being covered/turned off;

threads split between residents framing the cameras as surveillance/data tracking and

residents defending them for traffic enforcement and public safety, with some calling

for a public vote first. By October the conversation flipped in one notable thread to

residents demanding cameras where there were none. Moderation differed sharply by group.

  • - Cabot Strong, Aug 7, 2026 — A THV11 reporter posted seeking residents willing to be interviewed on camera about the Flock cameras being turned off (63 reactions, 121 comments). Comment themes included what would happen to footage already collected and arguments both for and against the cameras.
  • - Cabot Strong, Oct 24, 2026 — A resident posted angrily about vandalism at Richie Road park, asking why there were no cameras there (93 reactions, 120 comments). Commenters complained about graffiti and pressed for cameras mounted outside park facilities, with one tagging the mayor.
  • - WTF CABOT ARKANSAS, Sep 7, 2026 — An open debate prompt asked residents for their take on Flock cameras while the cameras sat covered (8 reactions, 40 comments). Supporters cited worsening driving behavior; opponents described the system as surveillance that tracks more than plates.
  • - Cabot's Petty Citizens – BCOC, Aug 8, 2026 — A moderation notice stated that posts about the cameras would be deleted (89 reactions, 64 comments). Commenters pushed back on free-speech grounds, while at least one cheered camera removal.

The earlier paper: Cabot Star-Herald

Cabot Star-Herald — research notes (Oct 26, 2026)

What it was

Cabot's long-running weekly, created by merging two papers:

  • - Cone and Betty Magie moved to Cabot and founded the Cabot Star in 1955

(a 2006 House memorial says the Star was started in 1954 and the Herald

bought in 1955 — small discrepancy, not resolved).

  • - Within months they bought the competing Cabot Herald and merged the two

into the Cabot Star-Herald (1956).

  • - Magie Enterprises stayed family-owned until Stephens Media Group bought

the company in 2006. Longtime publisher Cone Magie died in March 2006,

age 81. At the sale, the Cabot paper was the largest of the five Magie

papers, with a 12-month average circulation of about 7,769 (Arkansas

Press Association figure cited at the time).

  • - The paper is now defunct; its website is no longer reachable. One

low-quality page claims circulation "since 1904" — that does not fit the

Magie history and should not be used (1904 likely conflates a

predecessor title).

Predecessor and successor titles (Library of Congress catalog cards)

  • - The Cabot Guard — Cabot's first newspaper (1885 per the mayors-roster

research); LoC lineage runs to the Jacksonville Patriot (LCCN

SN92050005).

  • - The Cabot Herald — to 1956, when it merged into the Star-Herald; LoC

lineage runs to The AOP News (LCCN SN92050003).

  • - Cabot Star — to 1956, merged into the Star-Herald; LoC lineage runs to

the Jacksonville News (LCCN SN92050002).

  • - Cabot Star-Herald — LoC lineage runs to the Jacksonville Daily News

(LCCN SN92050001, OCLC 25119967). The exact end date of the Star-Herald

title and its relationship to the later Jacksonville titles needs

confirmation from the LoC catalog / WorldCat records, not from

secondary summaries.

Why it matters for the site

  • - It was Cabot's paper of record for roughly 50 years (1956–2006+),

covering the growth decades the Leader archive (2017+) does not reach.

  • - Cone Magie coined "Cabot, Coming Back Strong" after the March 29, 1976

tornado, printing it in large letters on an aluminum press sheet and

posting it in the downtown rubble — the origin story of the phrase, and

the best tornado-era material found so far (group mining found no 1976

reminiscence).

  • - The Encyclopedia of Arkansas Cabot entry cites a Star-Herald piece,

"Cabot Regarded as State's Only Boomtown" (Tyler Tucker, Feb 11, 2009),

so the title was still publishing in 2009.

Where archives may live (status: leads, not verified holdings)

  • - GenealogyBank / NewsBank: Cabot Star-Herald obituaries, Aug 22, 2007 –

May 2, 2017 (subscription; obituaries only, not full issues).

  • - Library of Congress catalog + WorldCat: title records and holdings

info for the Star-Herald and its predecessor/successor titles (see

LCCN/OCLC numbers above) — check these for microfilm locations.

  • - To check next: Arkansas State Archives newspaper microfilm holdings,

Cabot Public Library local-history collection, and CALS (Central

Arkansas Library System) collections. No free full-text digitized

archive of the Star-Herald was found in this pass.

  • - Open questions: exact final issue date; whether/how the Star-Herald

relates to the Arkansas Leader (the Leader's own founding date is not

yet established either).

Known limits

  • No free, full-text digitized run of the Cabot Star-Herald exists; the Lonoke County Library holds it on microfilm, in person only.
  • Mayors before 1960, and from 1979 to about 1995, are not identified in any public web source found — see the Mayors page.
  • Some scanned records are indexed by date and link before their OCR text is ready; the search page says which documents have full text.
