What Eddie Joe Williams Is Running On

Eddie Joe Williams — Cabot's mayor from 2007 to 2010, a former Arkansas state senator and majority leader, and since February 2026 the interim Lonoke County Clerk — is challenging Mayor Ken Kincade in the November 3, 2026 election. This page lays out his platform as he states it in his announcement, at the fall candidate forum, and on his campaign website; the record he points to from his earlier time in office; and the pushback and scrutiny his campaign has drawn, with his documented responses.

The story

Williams, 72, grew up in Sheridan and served in the Army in Korea and for nearly two years with the 189th Air National Guard. He spent more than 30 years with Union Pacific Railroad, starting as a laborer and rising through engineer and management to regional director of transportation for the eastern division, building a reputation, in his telling, as a "problem solver" sent to turn around troubled locations. He and his wife, DeLona — married 53 years, with four daughters and 11 grandchildren — settled in Cabot nearly 40 years ago during the railroad years so their daughters could attend a good high school.

His public career runs in layers: the Cabot planning commission and parks commission; three terms on the Cabot City Council; mayor from 2007 to 2010; election to the Arkansas Senate in 2010, where he served three terms until 2017, including as Senate majority leader; and a presidential appointment to the Southern States Energy Board, which he accepted in 2017 as a full-time Trump administration representative. After nine years retired from public office, he ran for Arkansas Secretary of State in 2022, challenging incumbent John Thurston in the Republican primary, and in January 2024 he served briefly as interim secretary of the state Department of Corrections.

The immediate road to the 2026 race ran through the county clerk's office. When Clerk Dawn Porterfield resigned on February 22, 2026, in the middle of the primaries, Williams contacted County Judge Doug Erwin offering to help; the quorum court appointed him interim clerk to finish the term. Joanie Howell won the office in the March Republican primary and takes over January 1, 2027, so Williams serves through December 31, 2026. He announced for mayor on July 29, 2026, the day filing opened, saying that since becoming clerk he had had hundreds of people ask him to run. At the Chamber of Commerce forum reported October 14, he and Kincade answered the same questions; on October 28 the Leader examined the race's two defining issues — debt and campaign money — on its front page.

One piece of context follows his candidacy everywhere: he is running for mayor while serving as the county's chief election administrator. His documented response is that on Election Day he will step back and let the clerk's office staff handle the ballots, that he will be careful not to frequent the Cabot polling places, and that a strong election commission and staff are in place.

What he's running on

His campaign website (goeddiejoe.com) organizes the run under the banner "Thoughtful Planning. Transparent Leadership. A Stronger Cabot," with four stated priorities. The Leader's July 29 announcement coverage summarized the same agenda as a focus on traffic, city debt, and bringing industry and jobs to Cabot.

1. Traffic — "Keeping Cabot Moving"

Williams argues Cabot's streets need major attention and that congestion is now bad enough that residents drive to Beebe or Jacksonville to shop — a point he repeated at the October forum. He campaigns on his 2007–2010 record: a third lane on West Main Street from Willie Ray Drive to Deer Creek (without which, he says, West Main would be chaos); turn lanes he added at Campground, Mount Carmel, and Kerr Station roads; a county-partnered turning lane on West Locust Street from Taco Bell to the Olive Street Y; and South Rockwood Road from Hwy. 5 to Walmart, built with the county, which he calls the highest-traffic-count road in Lonoke County. At the forum he also argued traffic cannot be managed simply intersection by intersection, light by light.

2. City debt and financial management — "A Strong Financial Future"

His signature issue. Citing the legislative audit, he says Cabot carries nearly $100 million in debt "hanging over our head," and he questions the breadth of consent behind it: in the 2021 special election that approved the $69 million bond issue, roughly 700 yes votes out of about 17,000 registered voters committed the city (figures from his review of the returns, published in the Leader, Oct. 28, 2026). Against that he sets his own mayoral record as he tells it: his administration inherited unpaid bills and debt, paid cash for Fire Station 2 on Hwy. 5 and staffed it, bought new fire trucks, and left office with $3–4 million in cash on hand for his successor. His pitch is managerial — he says he does not manage public money the way the current approach does, manages it as if it were his own, and offers experience, management, and root-cause analysis.

3. Jobs and industry — "Quality Jobs. Strong Economy"

Williams says there is no industry in Cabot and he would focus on helping existing employers — naming Dreamline Manufacturing and Pinnacle Structures — expand, while recruiting good-paying jobs, a call center, and high-tech manufacturing. He points to his Senate record: helping bring Sig Sauer to Arkansas as part of his district, working with the governor and the state Economic Development Commission on the package, and he connects that to D&M Holdings (ammunition-factory equipment and parts) locating in Cabot, saying he wants to help D&M find room to expand its powder propellant plant. From his mayoralty he cites working to bring a National Guard armory to Cabot and says builders want ease of doing business. His campaign site frames the goal as clean industry and quality employers so people can live, work, and invest in Cabot.

4. Business climate and transparency — "Helping Business Thrive"

The fourth website priority is that city government should work alongside business owners, reduce unnecessary barriers, and encourage investment. Layered over the platform is a transparency pledge: at the October forum he said he wants transparency in city government, and his announcement statement (carried by Arkansas Money & Politics) promises experienced leadership, thoughtful planning, and disciplined management — addressing traffic congestion, strengthening public safety, maintaining clean and welcoming neighborhoods, and supporting schools and businesses. His stated test for his own administration, offered at the forum, is an ethical one: he says he will never ask staff to do anything immoral, unethical, or unlawful, and that staff who believe an order crosses that line have his permission to refuse it.

The criticism he has drawn

  • - The debt framing. The Leader's Oct. 28 examination — the closest thing to a referee the race has — confirmed the overall debt figure but reported there is no evidence the bond issues have hurt the city's finances, and noted it is unclear whether Williams publicly opposed the 2021 bond issue before that election. Kincade's answer, at the forum and elsewhere: the bonds are being retired 18 years early (paid off in 2038) and are collateralized by the one-cent sales tax, "not city debt" in the general-fund sense.
  • - The past-mayors argument. At the forum, Kincade attributed today's congestion to poor management by past mayors who "created chaos" — saying no to a downtown overpass after the tornado, to city hall's location choices, to Walmart, and to several restaurants. Williams is the only past mayor on the ballot, though Kincade did not name him in the answer as reported.
  • - Money. By the Secretary of State filings the Leader reported on Oct. 28, Williams had brought in $38,625 — slightly more than Kincade's $34,350 — and had also taken out a $25,000 loan toward campaign expenses; he had spent $34,822 and held $28,954. Slightly more than $36,000 of his money came from individuals and $2,250 from businesses or PACs, per the same report.
  • - An older item. During his 2022 Secretary of State campaign, the Democrat-Gazette reported an ethics complaint alleging he had brought a vehicle displaying oversized campaign signs to the Capitol contrary to a 2015 law; Williams responded that he had forgotten the sign was on his car, that it happened once, and that it would not happen again. The article reported no final Ethics Commission outcome.
  • - In the Facebook groups, Williams barely registers: the October 26 mining and a fresh October 30 search pass found no substantive group discussion of his candidacy. The closest item was a July 29, 2026 BCOC post from a relative newcomer asking residents what they could say about "Eddie and Ken" — an open request for opinions about both men rather than a verdict on either.

What the record shows

  • - The biography checks out in its main lines. Mayor 2007–2010 after three council terms and commission service; Senate service from 2011 to 2017 with the majority-leader role; the Southern States Energy Board appointment; the 2024 interim corrections secretary appointment; and the February 2026 interim clerk appointment with a December 31, 2026 end date — each is confirmed by at least one of the Leader, the Democrat-Gazette, or Arkansas Money & Politics (see Sources). The railroad career (30-plus years, laborer to regional director) is as he and the Leader's profile describe it.
  • - His platform's factual anchors are partly verifiable, partly his account. The debt total he cites matches the Leader's own figure for outstanding bonded debt at the end of 2022 (about $99.65 million). The 2021 turnout numbers are his review of the returns, published without contradiction in the Oct. 28 Leader. The traffic projects he lists from his mayoralty, the $3–4 million cash figure, the Sig Sauer role, and the D&M Holdings connection are his statements, printed by the Leader; the sources reviewed for this page do not independently verify them, and they are presented here as his account. His campaign website's four priorities are stated in general terms — the sources reviewed contain no costed proposals or timelines attached to them.
  • - The clerk question is documented, and so is his answer. He is the interim clerk through the end of 2026 and the county's chief election administrator for the election he is contesting; his on-the-record commitments are to step back from ballot handling on Election Day and to stay away from Cabot polling places. Whether that is sufficient is a judgment for voters; no source reviewed reports any allegation of actual interference.
  • - What could not be verified: the Democrat-Gazette has no dedicated article on the 2026 Cabot mayoral race, so his platform cannot be cross-checked against a second newspaper's reporting — the sourcing is the Leader's announcement and forum coverage, Arkansas Money & Politics' announcement piece, and his own campaign site. His Senate-era claim to have fought low-turnout special elections is his statement of his record; the sources reviewed do not include the legislative history to test it.
