The Lyon College Deal: A Veterinary School for Cabot

The story

In November 2025, Cabot officials posed with a $5.7 million check: the land sale had closed, and a private college in Batesville was going to build a veterinary school — a $150 million campus with a teaching hospital — on city land along Willie Ray Drive. It is the largest single development deal in the city's history, and one of the most structurally complicated: the city sold the land to a developer, agreed to lease the campus back for 25 years, and subleased it to the college, in a structure designed so that, on paper, no tax revenue makes the city's lease payments. It is also a deal that changed shape on its way to closing — a different developer, a different parcel, and an opening date that has slipped twice.

How Lyon got to Cabot (2022–2024). Lyon College, a small private nonprofit college in Batesville, had planned a veterinary school at the Heifer International campus in Little Rock with OneHealth Education Group; that deal fell through in 2022. In 2023, Lyon and Cabot signed a memorandum of understanding to collaborate on community outreach, education and support services at the Cabot Animal Services Center — the animal shelter became the bridge between the college and the city. In May 2024 the council passed resolutions to enter lease agreements with a developer, American Development Partners of Nashville (also known as Jameson Holdings), and with Lyon College (council packets, May 6 and June 17, 2024).

  • - June 2024 — The council approves selling 14.4 acres for $5.5 million to American Development Partners to build the school near Ernie Davis Road, on part of the city's 220 acres along Willie Ray Drive near Game Time at the Grounds. The structure, as the Leader reported it (June 19, 2024): the city would enter a 25-year lease with the developer for the buildings, use a portion for a new Cabot Animal Support Services facility with a veterinary clinic, and sublease the rest to Lyon. Lyon's sublease payment would be set $50,000 a year above the city's lease payment, rising 2 percent annually; sublease money must go to the lease payment, with any surplus to the city's General Fund or Community and Economic Development Fund; tax revenues will not be used for the city's lease payments; and the council may decline to appropriate further lease payments at any time, for any reason. Mayor Kincade estimated the college would bring $30 million in economic development. The target was students by fall 2025. The agreements were published as legal notices that July.
  • - November 18, 2024 — The council "finalizes" the lease agreement (The Leader, Nov. 20, 2024) — but the deal has changed: the developer is now 11 Arrows Capital of Austin, Texas, described as chosen by Lyon College, and the parcel is now 19.22 acres at the same $5.7 million. The structure is otherwise as described in June. The council also passed a resolution to reimburse the Parks and Recreation Commission $700,000 from the land-sale proceeds, because the Grounds land had been developed with Parks' $21.7 million share of the 2021 bond program. Notably, the November coverage still said the design and cost of the project — and the cost of the lease and sublease themselves — were "to be determined." The agreement texts were published as legal notices in January 2025.
  • - February 12, 2025 — The designs are unveiled: a $150 million Lyon College Veterinary Teaching Hospital, with Cabot Animal Support Services housed in the same complex — third-year students would staff a clinic offering reduced- or no-cost animal services to the community.
  • - April 2, 2025 — The campus rezoning is approved.
  • - April 2025 — The Arkansas Animal Rescue Foundation gives $11 million to establish the Katharine Reese Shelter Medicine and Animal Welfare Program at the school — the largest outside validation the project had received. The college said the school planned to open in 2026, pending accreditation (The Leader, April 30, 2025).
  • - June 2025 — A ceremonial groundbreaking is held on the site (The Leader, June 11 and 18, 2025); the school is now described as opening to students in fall 2026.
  • - November 2025 — The land sale actually closes — 19.1 acres, $5.7 million — a year after it was "finalized" (The Leader, Nov. 12, 2025). Construction was to start that month and take about 14 months for the first building; classes are now expected to begin in 2027. The campus as described: multiple buildings across 14 acres, more than 100,000 square feet of classrooms, labs, surgical and simulation space, and a four-year doctor of veterinary medicine program. The coverage also recorded what the council had already approved doing with the money: part of the proceeds toward a $2.5 million indoor shooting range for the police department (plus $222,400 in engineering), with the remainder available to pay off a bond or fund future projects.
  • - 2026 — Construction proceeds along Willie Ray Drive. The administration campaigns on the school as a signature achievement (The Leader, Aug. 12, 2026). The Leader's Oct. 28, 2026 survey of city properties restated the structure — 25-year lease, sublease at $50,000 a year above the city's payment and rising 2 percent annually, surplus to city funds, the council's right to decline further appropriations — and put the opening in 2027. The same survey traced the animal-services thread: the standalone $4.7 million animal support building the 2021 bonds had funded for a site at Kerr Station Road and Adams Street was dropped in favor of co-locating with the college, and that land instead became the fire department's training tower and the police indoor shooting range.

What critics said

The striking fact about criticism of this deal is how little of it exists in the public record — especially set against the airport-grade fights Cabot has over cameras and sewer lines. What exists breaks down as follows:

  • - In the council and the paper: the Leader's coverage from 2024 through 2026 is almost entirely the story of officials announcing, approving, and celebrating steps in the deal. No organized opposition, no petition, no hearing-room confrontation appears in the corpus. The questions that do appear are embedded in the reporting itself: that the project's design, cost, and even the lease and sublease amounts were still "to be determined" at the November 2024 finalization; that the developer, acreage and timeline all changed between June 2024 and November 2025 without a public explanation in the news columns; and that a share of the proceeds was committed to a police shooting range. Whether the incentive-oversight scrutiny that hit the Olive Garden deal in September 2025 (see the Olive Garden page) will eventually be applied to a deal this much larger is a question the record so far leaves open.
  • - The mayor's opponents in 2026 have not made the deal an issue: Williams's campaign criticizes debt, traffic and incentives generally, but the sources reviewed contain no attack on the Lyon arrangement specifically — unsurprising, perhaps, for a project whose ribbon-cuttings feature both parties' constituencies (animal welfare, jobs, education). Kincade lists it among his signature achievements.

The group discourse: the sound of (almost) silence

Facebook mining was run twice for this topic — the project's October 26, 2026 pass and a fresh, thorough pass on Oct. 30, 2026 (terms: Lyon, vet school, veterinary, college) across Cabot Strong, WTF CABOT ARKANSAS and Cabot's Petty Citizens – BCOC. Both times the result was the same: no substantive resident discussion of the veterinary school deal exists in the groups. The queries return noise — lost pets, vet recommendations, school complaints — and exactly one on-point post: an August 14, 2026 BCOC post reporting two deceased dogs found on North Willie Ray Drive "just north of the new Cabot exit that goes to the upcoming vet school." The school appears in the discourse as a landmark — a way to give directions — not as a subject.

That absence is itself the finding, and it cuts two ways, both of which this page reports without choosing: either the deal genuinely commands the broad, quiet assent its supporters describe (animal welfare is one of the groups' warmest topics — a September 2026 Cabot Strong post about the animal services organization covering a homeless man's dog's medical bills drew 303 reactions and 136 comments of praise), or a complicated public-finance structure involving public land simply never got translated into a form the discourse could argue about, the way a camera on a pole or a sewer bill could be. The deal was approved, finalized, re-papered with a new developer, and closed across eighteen months of public meetings; the group record suggests almost nobody outside the process was watching.

What the record shows

  • - The structure, as documented at each step, is consistent; the parties and numbers around it moved. Developer: American Development Partners (May–June 2024, including the executed-style agreements published as legal notices in July 2024) → 11 Arrows Capital (November 2024 onward). Acreage: 14.4 acres (June 2024) → 19.22 acres (Nov. 2024) → 19.1 acres (closing, Nov. 2025), at $5.5 million → $5.7 million → $5.7 million. No source in the corpus explains the developer change or the acreage revisions; the November 2024 story introduces 11 Arrows simply as the developer Lyon chose. Anyone stating the deal's terms should say which version they mean.
  • - The opening date has slipped in print three times: fall 2025 (2024), fall 2026 (June 2025), classes beginning 2027 (Nov. 2025 and Oct. 2026). Some of that is the land closing a year after "finalization"; some depends on accreditation, which the April 2025 coverage flagged as still pending and which no source in the corpus shows as completed. The date to hold the project to, on current coverage, is 2027.
  • - The location is described inconsistently even by the same paper. The campus is consistently placed on the city's Willie Ray Drive tract near Ernie Davis Drive and the Grounds. But the Leader's 2021–2023 coverage calls the north interchange "Exit 21," while its November 2025 and 2026 Lyon stories place the campus "near I-57 Exit 29"; Arkansas Money & Politics described the land as "at exit 21." This page uses the street geography, which all sources agree on, and flags the exit numbers as an unresolved inconsistency in the coverage.
  • - The protections the city points to are real clauses, accurately reported: no tax revenues pledged to the lease payments; the sublease set above the lease payment with a 2 percent escalator; surplus sublease money to city funds; and the council's right to decline further appropriations at any time for any reason — the clause that most limits the city's long-term exposure. The counterweight, also in the record: the actual dollar amounts of the city's 25-year lease obligation were "to be determined" when the council finalized the deal in November 2024, and no later corpus source states the total. The $150 million figure is the project's stated cost (the college/developer's construction), not a city expenditure; the city's money in the deal is the land, the $700,000 Parks reimbursement it directed from proceeds, and the lease stream itself.
  • - The $30 million economic-impact figure is the mayor's estimate (June and November 2024), not a study quoted in the coverage. The $11 million AARF gift is documented (April 2025). The teaching-hospital/CASS co-location and the student clinic's reduced- or no-cost services are in the agreements as reported, not yet an operating fact.
  • - Unknown / gaps: why the developer changed; what the lease and sublease actually cost per year; the accreditation status as of late October 2026; whether the 14-month first-building schedule from November 2025 is holding; and what happens to the structure if enrollment or the college's finances disappoint — the non-appropriation clause protects the budget, but the corpus contains no discussion of a failure scenario.
