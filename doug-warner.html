Why Doug Warner resigned

The story

Doug Warner held Cabot's Ward 2, Position 1 council seat. He first appears in the council minutes on April 4, 2016, taking the seat Dallan Buchanan had vacated; by 2020 he was a senior member of the council, chairing its Budget and Personnel Committee in the 2020 agendas, and he was seeking a third term in the November 2020 election. He was 61, and outside city hall he was active in veterans' organizations.

Monday, October 5, 2020. Warner did not attend that evening's council work session. Instead, he had emailed his resignation letter to City Attorney Ben Hooper, who read it to the council; the council accepted the resignation (moved by Ed Long, seconded by Matt Webber, unanimously, per the minutes as summarized in this project's seat roster). In the same letter's wake, Warner also ended his re-election campaign, leaving Stephen Redd and Jayson Crumpler in the race. The seat then sat empty for the rest of the year — the December 14, 2020 minutes note plainly that the city had a vacancy in Ward 2, Position 1.

What Warner said, in his letter (as reported by the Leader, October 7, 2020):

  • - He was going through a divorce.
  • - For the previous weeks, claims about his personal life — some concerning events he said dated back as far as 30 years — had been circulated through conversations, social media posts, phone calls, emails and text messages, many of them, he said, from fake accounts, and in his account misrepresented.
  • - He acknowledged making questionable decisions in his personal life that he said were not against the law but went against some people's moral standards, and he maintained those issues had nothing to do with his council work, which he said should be judged on budgets, roads and economic development.
  • - The messages had reached organizations he belonged to or led, which depend on private donations, raising fears among donors; children had been drawn into a discussion that did not concern them; and careers and futures other than his own had been damaged, some possibly beyond repair.
  • - His conclusion was practical, not just personal: even if he won re-election, he expected the attacks to continue and other people to keep getting hurt, so he was resigning and ending the campaign. He asked that the messages stop and that other people's privacy be respected.

The ballot-box coda. Resigning did not remove Warner's name from the November 3, 2020 ballot — it was already set. In the three-way race he finished second: Stephen Redd 3,766 votes (39.8 percent), Warner 2,962, Jayson Crumpler 2,739. Because no candidate won a majority, the top two went to a runoff — and after the election, Warner announced he was resuming his campaign after all. The reversal did not hold at the ballot box: Redd won the December 1, 2020 runoff, 507–180. Redd was sworn in on January 1, 2021, filling the seat Warner had vacated three months earlier.

Afterward. Warner did not disappear from public life. The Leader shows him in 2023–2024 as commander of American Legion Post 91 in Beebe, presenting flags and colors at events in the Cabot area — the same veterans' circles in which he had been active while on the council (he appears in that role as early as 2019–2020 coverage).

What critics said

The criticism in this story is unusual: the campaign against Warner was itself the story, and it was conducted largely anonymously.

  • - The rumor campaign. By Warner's own account in his resignation letter, the chatter concerned his personal life and decades-old events, spread through social media posts and direct messages to him, his family, and organizations he was associated with, many from accounts he described as fake. That is the sum of what the record documents about its content. The specific allegations were never verified by any reporting this project found, no charge, lawsuit, or official finding arising from them appears in the record, and Warner described them as misrepresented. For those reasons, this page reports the themes — personal-life allegations, circulated anonymously, timed to an election — and does not repeat the claims themselves. Rumor is labeled here as rumor.
  • - The process criticism ran both ways. Warner's letter framed the episode as politics by anonymous attack: discussions, he argued, should be about budgets, roads and economic development, not gossip, and he pointed to collateral damage to family members, children, and donor-dependent organizations. Nothing in the coverage records a defense of the anonymous campaign by anyone willing to be named.
  • - What residents said, as far as it can still be checked. The contemporaneous Facebook threads themselves have not survived in retrievable form: targeted searches of the Cabot groups (Cabot's Petty Citizens – BCOC, Cabot Strong, and Cabot and Lonoke County News and Commentary) in October 2026 for Warner and the resignation surfaced no 2020 posts — group search is relevance-ranked and six-year-old threads no longer surface. So the surviving evidence of resident chatter is indirect: it is the campaign Warner's letter describes and responds to. Anyone reconstructing "what people said" in the fall of 2020 is, on this record, mostly reconstructing what Warner said people were saying.
  • - Press reaction. The Leader's treatment was straight news — the resignation led the paper's council coverage on October 7, 2020, alongside Q&As with the two remaining candidates — and follow-up issues (checked October 14 and 21, 2020) carried no editorial or further reporting on the resignation itself.

What the record shows

  • - Documented fact: Warner's seat (Ward 2, Position 1) and tenure (in the minutes from April 4, 2016); the October 5, 2020 letter, read aloud by the city attorney at the work session and accepted unanimously; the vacancy through December 31, 2020 (December 14, 2020 minutes); the November 3, 2020 results (Redd 3,766; Warner 2,962; Crumpler 2,739); Warner's post-election decision to resume campaigning (Leader, November 25, 2020); the December 1, 2020 runoff result (Redd 507, Warner 180); Redd sworn in January 1, 2021.
  • - His stated reason is the only on-record reason. The letter attributes the resignation to the personal and organizational damage from an anonymous rumor campaign during a divorce, and to his judgment that it would continue even if he won. Nothing in the council record — no censure, investigation, or ethics item — contradicts that account, and nothing in the corpus corroborates the underlying personal allegations either.
  • - What remains unknown:
  • - The truth of the personal-life rumors. They were unverified when circulated and remain unverified; Warner called them misrepresentations of old events.
  • - Who was behind the anonymous posts, emails and texts. No one was identified in any source found.
  • - How Warner first took the seat in 2016. The January–February 2016 minutes are missing from the city's archive, so whether he was appointed by the council or seated another way is not in the surviving record; he simply appears, seated, on April 4, 2016.
  • - Why he reversed himself after November 3 and contested the runoff he had withdrawn from. The Leader reported the resumption (November 25, 2020) without a stated reason that this research found.
